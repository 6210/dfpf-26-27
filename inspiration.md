It's called 𝘄𝗲𝗯𝘀𝗶𝘁𝗲-𝘁𝗼𝗼𝗹𝗸𝗶𝘁, and it follows a studio process, step by step:

𝟭. 𝗕𝗿𝗶𝗲𝗳 → What is the site for, what is the one job of the home page, three words for the vibe. Real content first, adjectives second.

𝟮. 𝗗𝗶𝗿𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 → Two or three distinct directions. Then reference screenshots get turned into principles: palette, type, grid, motion and one memorable element. Principles, never copies.

𝟯. 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗰𝗸 → Astro, static by default. One component library. One motion library. At most one heavy effect (3D, shader, particles) per page.

𝟰. 𝗕𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱 → Section by section, hero first, checked against the design spec as it goes.

𝟱. 𝗦𝗵𝗶𝗽 → Lighthouse mobile score of 90+, reduced-motion fallbacks, visible keyboard focus, layouts that hold up at 360 px.

🇪🇺 𝗣𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗮𝗰𝘆 𝗯𝘆 𝗱𝗲𝗳𝗮𝘂𝗹𝘁
Zero third-party requests at runtime. Fonts, scripts and 3D scenes are self-hosted, so no visitor IPs travel to a CDN and a font never becomes the reason you need a consent banner. Impressum and Datenschutzerklärung are on the checklist, and every library's license gets logged.

The biggest lesson from building it: great sites don't come from stacking effects. They come from restraint around one bold move.
